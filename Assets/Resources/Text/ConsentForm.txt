CONSENT TO PARTICIPATE IN A RESEARCH STUDY
 
Title of Research Study:         Trust and Cooperation with Social Service Robots Based on Humanlikeness in a Stressful Environment
 
Principal Investigator:         Raj Korpan, Ph.D.
                                       Assistant Professor
                                        Department of Computer Science, Hunter College
  



Overview
* You are being asked to join a research study. This form gives you important information that will help you decide whether or not to take part. It describes what would happen to you in the study, and helps you understand the reasons why you might or might not want to participate. 
* To participate, you must be 18 years or older and willing to participate.
* Approximately 100 individuals are expected to participate in the study.


Purpose
The purpose of this research is to examine how the human-likeness of social service robots influences trust and cooperation in a stressful and high-pressure environment. This research aims to contribute to the broader understanding of human-robot interactions, particularly in public and private social service settings, and to assess whether robot design reinforces or challenges existing social values regarding authority and service roles.


Procedures
If you agree to participate in this research study, we will ask you to do the following:  
* Interact with robots in a 3D simulated immersive environment. This will take place during the online study session for approximately 10-15 minutes.
* Complete survey measuring perceptions of acceptance, trust, comfort, predictability, safety, friendliness, naturalness, understanding, satisfaction, and sociability. The survey will be administered at the end of simulation and will take approximately 10-15 minutes.


Time Commitment
* Your participation in this research study consists of a simulation with a robot and a survey. Your total participation in this research study is expected to last for about 20-30 minutes.
 
Potential Risks or Discomforts
* We will do our best to protect the information we collect from you. However, there remains a risk that someone not involved in the research could access your data. The measures we are taking to reduce the risk are discussed in the Confidentiality section below. 
* Some of the questions the researchers ask you may be upsetting, or you may feel uncomfortable answering them. If you do not wish to answer a question, you can skip it and go to the next question.
* Research procedures described above may involve risks that cannot be anticipated at this time.   


Confidentiality 
* We will make our best efforts to maintain confidentiality of any information that is collected during this research study. We will protect your confidentiality by storing collected data in secure, password-protected, encrypted databases on institutional servers or encrypted external drives. Access to data will be restricted to research team members directly involved in the study. Access will be granted based on specific roles and responsibilities, and access logs will be maintained.
* The research team, authorized CUNY staff, and government agencies that oversee this type of research may have access to research data and records in order to monitor the research.  
* Publications and/or presentations that result from this study will not identify you by name.


Future Use
* We might use the information collected from you as part of this study for future research by the researchers of this study and by other researchers. Your information will have a code instead of your name or other information that could identify you. You will be asked later in this form about your choice of whether or not to allow this future use. 
         
Potential Benefits
* You will not directly benefit from your participation in this research study.
* The knowledge gained from this study may help with the understanding of how social service robots can be designed to foster trust and cooperation in public and high-pressure environments. By exploring how individuals assign blame and authority to robots, the research may enhance user experiences and build greater trust in robots.


Costs 
* There will be no cost to you if you participate in this research study.
 
Payment for Participation
* You will not receive any payment for participating in this research study.


New Information: 
If we learn of anything that may affect your decision to participate, we will inform you as soon as possible. You will then have a chance to reconsider your continuing participation in the research.
 
Participants’ Rights:  
* Your participation in this research study is entirely voluntary.  If you decide not to participate, there will be no penalty to you, and you will not lose any benefits to which you are otherwise entitled. 
* You can decide to withdraw your consent and stop participating in the research at any time, without any penalty.
* You are not giving up any of your legal rights by agreeing to participate.
